title: Vinaigrettes and home dressings

A vinaigrette is oil and acid held together. The standard ratio is three parts oil to one part acid. Lean towards two to one if the acid is sharp, such as red wine vinegar.

## Steps

1. Put the acid in a bowl or jar first: lemon juice or vinegar.
2. Add salt, pepper and flavour: crushed garlic, mustard, herbs, a little honey or maple syrup.
3. Add the oil slowly while whisking, or shake everything hard in a closed jar.
4. Taste on a leaf, not from the spoon. The leaf is the real test.
5. Correct with salt, acid or a pinch of sweetness.

## Creamy variants

- **Yoghurt dressing**: Greek yoghurt thinned with lemon juice and a splash of water, plus garlic and herbs.
- **Tahini dressing**: tahini, lemon juice, garlic and water. It thickens when you add acid and thins when you add water. Whisk in water a spoonful at a time.

## Storage

Dressings with fresh garlic or herbs keep 5 to 7 days in the fridge in a sealed jar. Oil-based dressings thicken in the cold, so take them out 10 minutes before use. Yoghurt dressings are best within 4 days.

## Common mistakes

- Pouring in all the oil at once. The dressing splits.
- Adding raw garlic to a dressing you keep for a week. The flavour grows harsh. Use less or grate it finely.
- Forgetting salt. Without it, even a good dressing tastes dull.
