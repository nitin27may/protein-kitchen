---
title: Tadka, tempering spices in hot oil
tags: [indian, spices, stove]
---

A tadka (tempering) is whole spices or aromatics sizzled briefly in hot oil or ghee and poured over a dish. It carries the fat-soluble flavour of the spices through the whole pot, and it is the step that makes dal taste like dal.

## Steps

1. Have everything measured and next to the stove. A tadka takes under a minute and cannot wait.
2. Heat the oil or ghee in a small pan over medium-high heat until a single cumin seed sizzles at once.
3. Add mustard seeds first, if using. Wait until they pop and slow down, about 20 to 30 seconds. Cover the pan with a lid for a moment, because they jump.
4. Add cumin seeds, curry leaves, dried chilli or hing. Stir for 10 seconds until the cumin darkens one shade and smells nutty.
5. Add garlic or ginger last, for 20 to 30 seconds, until just golden.
6. Pour it over the cooked dal or vegetables straight away. It should sizzle on contact. Cover for a minute so the flavour settles in.

## Cues

- Oil hot enough: a cumin seed sizzles and rises within 2 to 3 seconds.
- Ready to pour: spices are fragrant and a shade darker, never black.

## Common mistakes

- Oil too cool. The spices soak it up and taste raw.
- Oil too hot or leaving the pan on the heat. Burnt cumin and garlic turn bitter and cannot be fixed.
- Adding ground chilli powder to very hot oil. It scorches in seconds. Take the pan off the heat first.
- Skipping the lid. Hot mustard seeds spit oil.

Ghee gives the best flavour. For less saturated fat, use a teaspoon of ghee for flavour and finish with a neutral oil.
