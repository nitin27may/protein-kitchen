---
title: Steaming without a steamer
tags: [indian, steaming, stove]
---

Steaming cooks muthia, dhokla and vegetables with no added fat, so it suits a light, nutrient-dense plate. You do not need special equipment.

## Steps

1. Pick a pot with a lid and a rack: a steamer insert, a metal colander, or three balls of foil to lift a plate off the base.
2. Put 3 to 4 cm of water in the pot. The water must stay below the plate or rack.
3. Bring the water to a rolling boil before adding food. Cold-start steaming cooks unevenly.
4. Place the food on an oiled plate or in a perforated tray in a single layer, leaving gaps for steam.
5. Cover, keep the heat at medium-high and steam for the time in the recipe: 12 to 15 minutes for muthia logs, 5 to 8 minutes for cut vegetables.
6. Test with a knife or toothpick. It should come out clean for batter-based foods.
7. Turn off the heat and let it stand 2 minutes before lifting the lid, so water does not drip on the food.

## Cues

- Muthia or dhokla done: the surface springs back when pressed and the knife comes out clean.
- Vegetables done: bright colour and a knife slides in with slight resistance.

## Common mistakes

- Letting the water dry out. Top up with boiling water from a kettle.
- Opening the lid early. The batter sinks.
- Crowding the plate. The centre stays raw.

Steamed foods keep in the fridge for 3 days and reheat well in a covered pan with a spoonful of water.
