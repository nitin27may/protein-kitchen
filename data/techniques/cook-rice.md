---
title: Cooking rice that stays separate
tags: [indian, grains, stove]
---

Good rice is separate and fluffy, not gluey. The three controls are rinsing, the water ratio and the rest at the end.

## Steps

1. Measure rice by volume with a cup. Rinse in a bowl, swirling and draining, until the water is mostly clear, usually 3 rinses. Rinsing removes surface starch that makes rice sticky.
2. Soak basmati for 20 to 30 minutes if you have time. It cooks more evenly and grains stay longer.
3. Water for stovetop, as a guide: basmati about 1 part rice to 1.5 parts water, white long-grain about 1 to 1.75, brown rice about 1 to 2.25. Check the pack, because rice varies by brand and age.
4. Bring to a boil, add a pinch of salt, cover tightly and reduce to the lowest heat. Do not lift the lid.
5. Cook 12 to 15 minutes for white rice, 35 to 40 minutes for brown.
6. Turn off the heat and rest, covered, for 5 to 10 minutes. This finishes the steaming.
7. Fluff gently with a fork from the edge to the centre.

## Cues

- Done: all water is absorbed and small steam holes show on the surface.
- Too wet: uncover on low heat for 2 minutes. Too hard: sprinkle 2 tablespoons of hot water, cover and rest 5 minutes.

## Common mistakes

- Stirring while it cooks. It releases starch and makes it gluey.
- Skipping the rest, which leaves the top wet and the bottom hard.

Cooked rice must cool quickly and go in the fridge within 2 hours. Eat it within 1 day, and reheat until steaming hot. Rice left at room temperature is a food-safety risk.
