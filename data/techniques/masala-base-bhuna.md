---
title: Building a curry base (bhuna)
tags: [indian, stove, curry]
---

Most Indian curries share one base: onion, ginger, garlic and tomato cooked down until the oil separates. Doing this properly is the difference between a flat curry and a rich one, and it needs very little oil if you are patient.

## Steps

1. Heat the oil over medium heat and add whole spices (cumin) for 10 seconds.
2. Add chopped onion with a pinch of salt. Cook, stirring every minute or so, 6 to 8 minutes for translucent and 10 to 12 for golden brown. Splash a spoonful of water if it sticks, instead of adding more oil.
3. Add ginger and garlic for 1 minute, until the raw smell is gone.
4. Add ground spices (turmeric, coriander, chilli, garam masala) and stir for 20 seconds. Add a splash of water if they stick. Never leave ground spices dry in a hot pan.
5. Add chopped tomato. Cook on medium, pressing the tomato down, 6 to 10 minutes until it breaks into a thick paste.
6. Stop when you see oil shimmering at the edges of the paste. That is the cue that the base is ready for the main ingredient.

## Cues

- Onion ready: golden brown at the edges and sweet smelling.
- Tomato ready: no watery liquid left, paste holds a trail when you drag the spoon.

## Common mistakes

- Rushing the onion on high heat. It burns outside and stays raw inside.
- Adding water too soon. The base steams instead of frying and tastes sharp.
- Adding paneer or tofu to a watery base. It dilutes the sauce.

The base freezes well in an ice-cube tray. Make a double batch on Sunday and drop two cubes into a pan for a quick curry.
