---
title: Pressure cooker basics for dal, beans and rice
tags: [indian, legumes, pressure-cooker]
---

A pressure cooker cuts cooking time for dal and beans by two thirds and uses little attention. Whistle counts vary between cookers, so use them as a guide and trust texture.

## Steps

1. Rinse dal until the water runs nearly clear. Soak rajma, chole and whole moong overnight and discard the soaking water.
2. Fill the cooker no more than two thirds full. For beans and dal that foam, no more than half.
3. Water: about 3 cups for 1 cup of split dal, and about 3 cups for 1 cup of soaked beans. Add turmeric and a few drops of oil, which also reduces foam.
4. Close the lid, bring to full pressure on high, then lower the heat to medium.
5. Cooking times after the first whistle: masoor and moong dal 2 to 3 whistles, toor and chana dal 4 to 5, soaked rajma and chole 6 to 8 (20 to 30 minutes).
6. Turn off the heat and let the pressure release on its own, about 10 to 15 minutes. Open only when the pin has dropped.
7. Mash the dal with a whisk. Beans should crush easily between two fingers.

## Cues

- Done: no firm centre and no grit when you press a bean.
- Not done: close and cook 5 more minutes, then check again.

## Safety

- Never force the lid open. Never fill past the marked line.
- Kidney beans must be boiled hard for at least 10 minutes. Full pressure covers that.
- Clean the vent and gasket after every use, because dal foam clogs them.

Salt can go in at the start for beans and dal. Add tomato and tamarind after cooking if the beans are still firm, since acid slows softening.
